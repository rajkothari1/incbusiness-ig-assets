# #001 — Mamaearth (Honasa Consumer Ltd)

**Series:** 100 Indian Business Models
**Status:** Draft v3: storytelling version (10 slides), copy only
**Data as of:** FY26 (Apr 2025 – Mar 2026), results declared May 2026
**Rule:** every claim on a slide has at least 2 independent sources. Claims with only 1 source were left out.

---

## Slide 1 — Hook

**In 2014, two new parents couldn't find one safe baby lotion in India.**
**Today their company makes ₹2,392 Cr a year.**

This is the Mamaearth story 👉

## Slide 2 — The problem at home

In 2014, Varun and Ghazal Alagh had their first son, Agastya.

Varun was a brand manager who had worked at **HUL, Diageo and Coca-Cola**. He knew how big FMCG brands work. But the two of them **couldn't find toxin-free baby products in India**.

Their workaround: ask friends and family to **bring them from the US**. It was costly and slow.

One question stuck with them: *why isn't this made in India?*

## Slide 3 — Starting small

**2016:** they launched Mamaearth with just **6 toxin-free baby products**.

There was no big VC cheque. They put in **₹30 lakh of their own savings**, plus **₹60 lakh from friends & family**.

To win the trust of anxious parents, they got **MADE SAFE certification**, a first for any brand in Asia.

## Slide 4 — Marketing on a tiny budget

This was before Instagram influencers. So Ghazal **made friends with mom bloggers**.

> "When a mother tells another mother of an experience, that has the most trust." (Ghazal Alagh)

Instead of ads, they spent money on **free samples**:
> "The cheapest way to market a product is to make our consumers experience the product."

## Slide 5 — "What about us?"

Parents loved the baby range and started asking: *why only babies? We want toxin-free products too.*

**2017:** Mamaearth expanded into **adult skin & hair care**.
**2018:** Shilpa Shetty joined as **brand ambassador and investor**.

Revenue took off:
**FY20: ~₹110 Cr → FY21: ₹461 Cr → FY22: ₹943 Cr**

## Slide 6 — Unicorn & Shark Tank

**Jan 2022:** raised **$52M led by Sequoia at a $1.2B valuation**, making it **India's first unicorn of 2022**.

Ghazal became a **judge on Shark Tank India**, and Mamaearth became a household name.

Parent company **Honasa** turned into a house of brands: The Derma Co, Aqualogica, Dr. Sheth's, BBlunt, Staze…

## Slide 7 — The business model

**Own the brand. Outsource the factory.**

1. Honasa owns the brand, the formulas & the customer
2. **Third-party contract manufacturers** make the products
3. Sold everywhere: marketplaces, quick commerce, its own website & shops

The biggest lever is marketing. **32.9% of revenue went to advertising** in Q4 FY26, nearly ₹1 of every ₹3 earned.

## Slide 8 — The IPO everyone doubted

**Nov 2023:** IPO at **₹324/share**, valuing Honasa at about **₹10,500 Cr**.

Analysts called it expensive: Honasa had posted a **₹151 Cr loss in FY23**, mostly from writing off the ₹154 Cr it paid for Momspresso.

By day 2 the IPO was only **30% subscribed**. It closed **7.6x subscribed**.

## Slide 9 — The crash

**Nov 2024:** Honasa reported its **first quarterly loss (~₹18.6 Cr)**. Distributors were holding too much stock.

The stock **fell 20% in a single day**, below the IPO price.

**The fix, "Project Neev":** remove the super-stockist layer and appoint **direct distributors in India's top 50 cities**.

## Slide 10 — The comeback (FY26)

Revenue journey: FY20 ₹110 Cr → FY21 ₹461 Cr → FY22 ₹943 Cr → FY23 ₹1,492 Cr → FY24 ₹1,920 Cr → FY25 ₹2,067 Cr → **FY26 ₹2,392 Cr**

- Revenue **₹2,392 Cr** (+16%)
- Net profit **₹200 Cr** (FY25: ₹73 Cr, about 2.75x)
- Younger brands grew **40%+**
- **First-ever dividend: ₹3/share**

*From 6 baby products to a ₹2,392 Cr house of brands.*
