# Apollo Hospitals Business Model: How Apollo Makes Money (2026)

Apollo Hospitals opened its first hospital in Chennai in 1983. In FY26, Apollo Hospitals revenue reached ₹25,229 crore. This Apollo Hospitals case study breaks down how Apollo Hospitals makes money from hospitals, pharmacies and digital health.

The model in one line: **Apollo treats patients in its hospitals, sells medicines through 7,000+ pharmacies, and connects both through its Apollo 24/7 app.**

## About Apollo Hospitals

| | |
|---|---|
| Founded | 1983, Chennai |
| Founder | Dr. Prathap C. Reddy |
| First hospital | 150 beds, Greams Road, Chennai |
| Claim to fame | India's first corporate hospital |
| Listed company | Apollo Hospitals Enterprise Ltd (NSE: APOLLOHOSP) |
| Network | 70+ hospitals, 10,000+ beds, 7,289 pharmacies |

**The problem:** in 1979, Dr. Reddy's 38-year-old patient needed heart surgery in the US. The family couldn't afford to send him, and he died. Dr. Reddy decided India needed world-class hospitals of its own.

## Apollo Hospitals Target Audience

- **Patients who need advanced care:** heart surgery, cancer treatment, transplants and complex procedures
- **International patients** who come to India for treatment
- **Everyday customers** buying medicines in stores or on the app
- **Healthy people** who want regular health checks (Apollo ProHealth)

## Apollo Hospitals Business Model

**1. Hospitals (Healthcare Services).** The core business. Apollo earns from surgeries, treatments, room stays, tests and doctor visits.

**2. Pharmacy network.** 7,289 Apollo Pharmacy stores (March 2026). The Apollo Pharmacy business model is simple: high-footfall stores near hospitals and homes, selling medicines every day.

**3. Apollo 24/7 (digital).** An app for online doctor consultations, medicine delivery and lab tests.

**4. Apollo Health & Lifestyle (AHLL).** Clinics, diagnostics and other smaller-format health centres outside big hospitals.

**5. The ecosystem.** One patient can use all four: a hospital surgery, follow-up medicines from the pharmacy, video check-ups on Apollo 24/7, and yearly health checks.

| Business | What it sells | Share of revenue (Q4 FY26) |
|---|---|---|
| Hospitals | Treatment, surgery, tests | ~49% |
| Apollo HealthCo | Pharmacy + Apollo 24/7 | ~43% |
| AHLL | Clinics, diagnostics | ~7% |

## How Does Apollo Hospitals Earn Money?

**Hospitals earn from treatment. Pharmacies and the app earn from selling medicines.**

| Metric | FY25 | FY26 |
|---|---|---|
| Revenue | ₹21,794 Cr | ₹25,229 Cr (+16%) |
| EBITDA | ₹3,033 Cr | ₹3,769 Cr (+24%) |
| Net profit | — | ₹1,942 Cr (+34%) |
| Final dividend | — | ₹10 per share |

**What drives hospital revenue:** more patients, more complex (higher-value) cases and higher prices. In Q4 FY26, hospital revenue grew 16% to ₹3,268 Cr.

## The Big Move: Apollo Healthtech Demerger

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| 2024 | Advent International invests **₹2,475 Cr for a 12.1% stake** in Apollo's pharmacy arm |
| 2025 | Plan announced: pharmacy + Apollo 24/7 to be demerged into **Apollo Healthtech Ltd** |
| 2025 | **Keimed**, India's largest pharma wholesaler, to merge into the same company |
| 2025 | Competition Commission of India (CCI) approves the restructuring |
| Target | ~₹25,000 Cr revenue at listing; listing by **Q4 FY27** |

**Why it matters:** Apollo Hospitals becomes a pure hospital company. Medicines and digital health get their own listed company, which can raise its own money. Apollo Hospitals keeps a minority stake.

## Conclusion

Apollo's playbook: **win patients' trust with big hospitals, then serve the same patients for life through pharmacies, an app and health checks.** The next chapter is two focused companies, one for hospitals and one for medicines and digital health.
