# Mamaearth Business Model Explained: How Honasa Makes ₹2,392 Crore a Year

Mamaearth started in 2016 as a small brand of toxin-free baby products. Ten years later, its parent company, Honasa Consumer Ltd, made ₹2,392 crore in revenue in FY26 (April 2025 to March 2026). It also earned ₹200 crore in profit. This guide uses Honasa Consumer revenue and profit figures from its official yearly results.

In simple words, the **Mamaearth business model** works like this. Honasa creates and markets the brands, outside factories make the products, and the products are sold online and in shops across India. This is often called a **digital-first, asset-light** model. Asset-light means the company owns very few heavy things, like factories.

This guide explains how Mamaearth makes money, where that money goes, and why the model works. Every number here comes from company results, checked against at least two news reports.

## How Mamaearth Started: A Problem at Home

In 2014, Varun and Ghazal Alagh had their first son. They wanted baby products without harsh chemicals, but they could not find them in Indian shops. Friends and family had to bring products from the US, which was slow and costly.

Varun had worked as a brand manager at Hindustan Unilever, Diageo and Coca-Cola, so he knew how big consumer brands are built. In 2016, the couple launched Mamaearth with just six toxin-free baby products. They started with about ₹30 lakh of their own savings and ₹60 lakh from friends and family.

Parents liked the products and soon asked for safe products for themselves. So in 2017, Mamaearth moved into adult skin care and hair care. That one step turned a baby brand into a full personal care company.

Growth came quickly after that. In January 2022, Honasa raised **$52 million** in a round led by Sequoia at a $1.2 billion valuation, making it India's first unicorn of 2022. Ghazal Alagh also became a judge on Shark Tank India, which made the founders household names.

In November 2023, Honasa listed on the stock market at ₹324 per share. Many analysts felt the price was too high, but the IPO was still subscribed about 7.6 times.

## What Is the Mamaearth Business Model?

The Mamaearth business model has three main parts: asset-light manufacturing, omnichannel selling, and a house of brands. Honasa Consumer Ltd is the parent company that owns Mamaearth and its sister brands. **Omnichannel** simply means selling through many channels at once, both online and offline.

Think of it like this. Honasa decides what to make, creates the formula, builds the brand and finds the customer. Partner factories do the manufacturing. The finished products then reach buyers through apps, websites, marketplaces and local shops.

> "Own the brand, rent the factory." — a common way to describe the asset-light model

This model lets a young company grow fast without spending years and crores of rupees on building its own plants. It also keeps the team focused on what customers want, which is where most of the value is created.

## Asset-Light Manufacturing: Owning the Formula, Not the Factory

Mamaearth's products are designed by an in-house team of about 50 people, led by co-founder Ghazal Alagh. This team works on new ideas, ingredients and formulas. The actual production is done by **contract manufacturers**, which are outside factories paid to make products for the brand.

In 2024, Honasa bought the assets of Cosmogenesis. These included an R&D lab with more than 4,000 formulations and a small manufacturing unit. So it is not correct to say that Honasa owns no factory at all today. Still, most of its production happens at partner factories.

Why does this matter? Factories need a lot of money and time to build. By skipping that, Honasa can spend more on new products and marketing. It can also test a product in small batches and only make more if people like it.

## Omnichannel Distribution: Online First, Now Everywhere

Mamaearth began as an online brand. It sold through its own website and app, and through marketplaces like Amazon, Flipkart, Nykaa and FirstCry. Later, it also joined quick commerce apps like Zepto and Swiggy Instamart, which deliver in minutes.

Over time, shops became a much bigger part of the business. According to Honasa's IPO documents, offline sales grew from about 9% of revenue in FY20 to about 29% in FY22. By June 2026, its products were reaching around **3 lakh retail outlets** across India.

Selling in shops was not always smooth. In late 2024, Honasa found that its distributors were holding too much stock. It reported its first quarterly loss, of about ₹18.6 crore, and its share price fell 20% in a single day.

The fix was called **Project Neev**. Honasa removed a middle layer of large wholesalers, called super-stockists, and appointed direct distributors in India's top 50 cities. This gave the company better control over how much stock sat in the market.

## The House of Brands Strategy

Honasa does not depend on just one brand. It runs a **house of brands**, where each brand speaks to a different type of customer. This spreads the risk and helps the company reach more buyers with different needs.

| Brand | What it focuses on | Who it is for |
|---|---|---|
| Mamaearth | Natural, toxin-free personal care | Families and young adults |
| The Derma Co | Skincare with active ingredients | People who want science-based skincare |
| Aqualogica | Skincare that adds moisture | Young skincare buyers |
| Dr. Sheth's | Skincare made for Indian skin | People with Indian skin concerns |
| BBlunt | Salon-style hair care and styling | People who style their hair |
| Staze | Makeup | Makeup buyers |

Honasa bought BBlunt and Dr. Sheth's in 2022. In FY26, its younger brands grew more than 40%, faster than Mamaearth itself. This shows that the house of brands is now one of the company's main growth engines.

There is a simple logic behind this. One brand cannot be natural, scientific, salon-style and budget-friendly all at once. Separate brands let Honasa target each group clearly, while sharing the same team, suppliers and sales network behind the scenes.

## Value Proposition: Toxin-Free Products and Fast Launches

Mamaearth's main promise is safe, **toxin-free** products. This mattered because many young parents were reading ingredient labels and worrying about chemicals. It was Asia's first brand to get **MADE SAFE** certification, a label from a US non-profit for products made without known harmful chemicals. Many of its products are sold as free from parabens, sulphates and SLS, though the exact claim changes from product to product.

The second part of the promise is speed. Honasa watches what customers search for and talk about online, then launches products quickly. It launched 122 new products in 2023. Recently launched products brought in about 9% of revenue in the April to June 2024 quarter.

Popular ingredients like onion, rice water and vitamin C became full product ranges. The Onion hair care range, for example, was promoted with Sara Ali Khan in national TV ads. If a product sells well, the company makes more of it. If it does not, the team moves on to the next idea.

## How Mamaearth Makes Money

Mamaearth makes money in a simple way: it sells products. Its revenue comes from skin care, hair care, baby care and other personal care items, sold online and in shops.

The products themselves earn a high margin. Honasa's **gross margin** was about 70% in the January to March 2026 quarter. Gross margin is the money left after paying the cost of making the product. In plain terms, products that sell for ₹100 cost about ₹30 to make.

But a big part of the remaining money goes to advertising. In the same quarter, ads took **32.9% of revenue**, or about ₹33 of every ₹100. After ads, salaries and other costs, Honasa earned a net profit of ₹200 crore on ₹2,392 crore of revenue in FY26. That is a **net margin** of roughly 8%.

The Mamaearth revenue FY26 figure also needs one note. A change in how the Flipkart group settles payments lowered Honasa's reported revenue by about ₹87 crore in FY26, without changing its profit. So the real sales growth was a little higher than the reported 16%.

FY26 was a comeback year. Revenue grew **16%** from ₹2,067 crore in FY25, and net profit rose from ₹73 crore to **₹200 crore**, about 2.75 times higher. The company also paid its **first-ever dividend** of ₹3 per share. Over six years, revenue has grown from about ₹110 crore in FY20 to ₹2,392 crore in FY26.

## Marketing and Growth Engine

In the early days, Mamaearth had a small budget, so it relied on word of mouth. Ghazal Alagh made friends with mom bloggers, because new mothers trust other mothers more than ads.

> "When a mother tells another mother of experience, that has the most trust." — Ghazal Alagh, speaking to Storyboard18

She also explained why bloggers mattered so much back then, before influencer marketing took off.

> "At that time, there was no Instagram but bloggers." — Ghazal Alagh, via Business Today

The company also gave away free samples, so people could try a product before paying for it. As Mamaearth grew, it added celebrities for wider reach. Shilpa Shetty joined as brand ambassador and investor in 2018, and Sara Ali Khan became the face of its hair care range in 2021.

Mamaearth also uses cause marketing, which means linking the brand to a good cause. Under its Plant Goodness campaign, it plants a tree for every order placed on its website and app. It has planted over **8 lakh trees** so far, which supports its natural and eco-friendly image.

## Frequently Asked Questions

**What is the business model of Mamaearth?**
Mamaearth follows a digital-first, asset-light, omnichannel model. Its parent company, Honasa Consumer Ltd, designs and markets the products, contract manufacturers make most of them, and they are sold online and in about 3 lakh shops across India.

**Who owns Mamaearth?**
Mamaearth is owned by Honasa Consumer Ltd, a listed company founded by Varun Alagh and Ghazal Alagh in 2016. Honasa also owns The Derma Co, Aqualogica, Dr. Sheth's, BBlunt and Staze.

**What is Mamaearth's revenue?**
Honasa Consumer reported revenue from operations of ₹2,392 crore in FY26 (April 2025 to March 2026), up 16% from ₹2,067 crore in FY25. Its net profit for FY26 was ₹200 crore.

**Does Mamaearth make its own products?**
Mostly no. Mamaearth creates its formulas in-house but uses third-party contract manufacturers to make most of its products. In 2024, Honasa bought an R&D lab and a small manufacturing unit from Cosmogenesis.

## The Bottom Line

Mamaearth grew from six baby products into a ₹2,392 crore company by keeping its model simple: own the brand and the customer, and let partners handle the factories. Its house of brands and its push into shops now drive growth, while heavy advertising remains its biggest challenge to profit. For anyone studying the D2C business model India has built over the last ten years, this Mamaearth case study is one of the clearest examples. It shows how a brand can start online, win trust with a real promise, and then grow into shops and new brands step by step.
