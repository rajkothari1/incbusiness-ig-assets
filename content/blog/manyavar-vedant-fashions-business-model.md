# Manyavar Business Model Explained

Manyavar started in 1999 with ₹10,000 borrowed from Ravi Modi's mother. Today its parent, Vedant Fashions Ltd, runs 669 stores and earned ₹1,436 crore in revenue in FY26 at a 26% profit margin.

This Manyavar case study explains the Vedant Fashions business model and how Manyavar makes money.

**The model in one line:** Vedant designs and brands wedding wear; franchise partners own and run the stores; Vedant earns on every piece they sell.

## The Problem Manyavar Solved

Indian men's wedding wear was **unorganised**: local tailors, small shops, no trusted brand and no fixed prices.

Ravi Modi grew up working in his father's small clothing shop in Kolkata's AC Market. He borrowed ₹10,000 from his mother and started making his own kurtas, first selling to multi-brand shops.

His idea: give men one trusted brand for every wedding and festival, with the same design, quality and price in every city.

## Manyavar Target Audience

- **Grooms and wedding guests** who want a sherwani, kurta or Indo-western outfit
- **Families shopping together** for weddings: men, women and kids
- **Festive shoppers** for Diwali, Eid and other celebrations
- **The Indian diaspora** abroad, through international stores

## Manyavar Business Model

**1. Franchise-led stores.** The Manyavar franchise model is the heart of the business: most Exclusive Brand Outlets (EBOs) are owned and run by franchise partners. Vedant spends less money on stores and grows faster.

**2. Brand, design and supply chain stay with Vedant.** It designs new collections all year (a "52-week" design model) and refills each store automatically, down to pin-code level.

**3. One price everywhere.** The same price online and offline, across India.

**4. No discounts.** Manyavar has **no end-of-season sales**, so customers don't wait for a sale and stores don't dump old stock.

**5. A brand for every budget.**

| Brand | Since | For | Price range |
|---|---|---|---|
| Manyavar | 1999 | Men & boys | Mid-premium |
| Mohey | 2015 | Women | Mid-premium |
| Mebaz | Acquired 2017 | Whole family, South India | Mid-premium to premium |
| Twamev | 2019 | Men & women | Premium |
| Diwas | 2024 | Men, festive wear | Value to mid-premium |

## How Manyavar Makes Money

Vedant Fashions earns from **selling its clothes through four channels**:

| Channel | How it works |
|---|---|
| **Exclusive Brand Outlets (EBOs)** | The main channel: franchise partners run Manyavar, Mohey, Twamev and Mebaz stores |
| **Multi-brand outlets (MBOs) & shop-in-shops** | Manyavar and Diwas sold inside other clothing stores |
| **Large format stores (LFS)** | Big department stores |
| **Online** | Its own website and leading e-commerce platforms |

**Two numbers to know:**

| FY26 | |
|---|---|
| **Retail sales** (what shoppers paid at the stores) | ₹2,008 Cr |
| **Revenue** (what Vedant earned from selling to its channel partners) | ₹1,436 Cr |

Retail sales show what shoppers spent across all its channels. Revenue is what Vedant itself books, mainly from selling to its franchise and channel partners.

**What it earns on each sale:**
- **Gross margin ~66%:** after paying for the clothes, about two-thirds of revenue is left
- **Profit margin 26%:** ₹376 Cr profit on ₹1,436 Cr revenue in FY26

## Manyavar's Ups and Downs

| When | What happened |
|---|---|
| 1999 | Manyavar starts with ₹10,000 from Ravi Modi's mother |

| FY23–FY26 | Revenue nearly flat; profit falls from ₹429 Cr to ₹376 Cr |
| Q3 FY26 | Fewer wedding dates in December and none in January hit sales |
| 2024–26 | Diwas launched; push into women's, premium and festive wear |

## Conclusion

Manyavar's playbook: **own the brand and the supply chain, let franchise partners own the stores, and never discount.** That gives it industry-leading margins. Its challenge is growth: sales depend on the wedding calendar, and revenue has barely moved since FY23.
