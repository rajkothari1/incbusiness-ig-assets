# India's Top 100 Business Models: company list

Status: Reel = episode made · Blog = website blog written. Update this file after each episode.

**Consumer internet and commerce (20)**
1. Eternal (Zomato + Blinkit): commission from food delivery, plus quick commerce that now holds its own stock
2. Swiggy (Instamart): delivery platform that earns from commissions, delivery fees and ads
3. Zepto: 10-minute delivery from its own "dark store" warehouses
4. Flipkart: marketplace earning commissions, ad money and logistics fees
5. Meesho: charges sellers zero commission and makes money from ads and logistics
6. Nykaa: buys and holds its own stock of beauty products, with a trust-led brand
7. FirstCry: one-stop store for kids' products, online and in shops
8. Lenskart: makes its own glasses and sells them online and in stores
9. BigBasket: grocery delivery owned by Tata
10. Urban Company: marketplace for home services with standardised quality
11. IndiaMART: B2B listings site that charges sellers a subscription
12. Justdial: local search that sells paid listings
13. Info Edge (Naukri): job listings plus a portfolio of startup investments
14. Cars24: buys and resells used cars
15. MakeMyTrip: online travel agency earning commission on bookings
16. ixigo: travel app focused on train tickets for smaller cities
17. OYO: brings budget hotels under one brand through franchising
18. Rapido: drivers pay a flat subscription instead of commission per ride
19. Ola: ride-hailing marketplace
20. Delhivery: logistics network shared across many sellers

**Fintech and finance (13)**
21. Paytm: payments used to attract customers, then earns by distributing loans
22. PhonePe: free UPI payments, earning from loans, insurance and investments
23. Zerodha: discount broker that spends nothing on advertising
24. Groww: investing app aimed at first-time investors
25. CRED: rewards users for paying credit card bills, earns from a high-credit-score audience
26. Razorpay: payment gateway, now adding more business services
27. Pine Labs: card machines at shops plus "buy now, pay later" at checkout
28. Policybazaar: insurance comparison site that earns commission
29. HDFC Bank: earns on the gap between low-cost deposits and loan interest
30. Bajaj Finance: consumer loans issued right at the shop counter
31. Muthoot Finance: gold loans
32. LIC: insurance sold through a huge network of agents
33. MobiKwik: payments plus small loans

**Tech, SaaS and IT (5)**
34. TCS: IT services delivered from India at lower cost, billed by the hour or by the project
35. Infosys: IT services, plus its consulting push
36. Zoho: software company built without outside funding, offices in rural India
37. Freshworks: SaaS built in India for global customers
38. Dixon Technologies: manufactures electronics for other brands

**Conglomerates, telecom and infrastructure (7)**
39. Reliance Industries: oil and chemicals profits funding consumer businesses
40. Jio: free data to win users first, then raise prices
41. Bharti Airtel: growth by raising revenue per user (ARPU)
42. Larsen & Toubro: engineering and construction contracts (EPC)
43. Adani Ports: ports that earn steady fees, much like a toll road
44. UltraTech Cement: wins on scale and a large dealer network
45. Waaree Energies: solar panel manufacturing helped by import protection

**Edtech, media and gaming (7)**
46. PhysicsWallah: very low-priced test prep that started on YouTube
47. upGrad: courses for working professionals
48. Dream11: fantasy sports, now pivoting after the 2025 ban on real-money gaming
49. Pocket FM: audio series where listeners pay a few rupees per episode
50. Saregama: owns a large catalogue of old songs and licenses it
51. PVR INOX: makes much of its money from popcorn and ads, not only tickets
52. Nazara: buys up gaming companies

**FMCG, food and D2C (17)**
53. Hindustan Unilever: distribution that reaches millions of small shops
54. ITC: cigarette profits funding its FMCG business
55. Amul: cooperative owned by its farmers
56. Haldiram's: traditional snacks brand built by a family business
57. Britannia: products at fixed low price points like ₹5 and ₹10
58. Patanjali: Ayurveda and "swadeshi" brand built around a founder
59. Lijjat Papad: women's cooperative
60. Jubilant FoodWorks: runs Domino's in India under a franchise
61. Rebel Foods: delivery-only cloud kitchens running many brands
62. Licious: meat brand that controls its own supply chain
63. Wow! Momo: fast food chain scaling Indian street food
64. Pidilite (Fevicol): created its own category and built a moat through carpenters
65. Asian Paints: dealer network plus paint mixing at the store
66. Honasa (Mamaearth): FMCG brand that started online and markets through influencers
67. Minimalist: D2C skincare brand bought by HUL
68. boAt: outsources manufacturing and wins on marketing
69. Wakefit: D2C mattresses and furniture

**Retail, fashion and jewellery (6)**
70. DMart: owns its stores and keeps prices low every day
71. Trent (Zudio): own-brand fashion at very low prices
72. Titan (Tanishq): trust and standard purity in a disorganised jewellery market
73. Kalyan Jewellers: store franchise model (FOCO)
74. Vedant Fashions (Manyavar): wedding wear sold through franchise stores
75. Page Industries: holds the Jockey licence in India

**Auto, mobility and travel (9)**
76. Maruti Suzuki: mass-market cars plus a huge service network
77. Mahindra & Mahindra: SUVs and tractors
78. Eicher (Royal Enfield): owns the premium motorcycle niche
79. Bajaj Auto: export-led two-wheelers
80. Ola Electric: vertically integrated electric scooters
81. Ather Energy: premium electric scooters with its own charging network
82. IndiGo: low-cost airline running a single type of aircraft
83. IRCTC: monopoly on online train tickets, earning convenience fees
84. BluSmart: electric cab company that collapsed (cautionary tale)

**Healthcare (5)**
85. Apollo Hospitals: hospitals plus a pharmacy network
86. Narayana Health: low-cost heart surgery at large volume
87. Sun Pharma: generic medicines plus speciality drugs
88. Mankind Pharma: generics sold to doctors in rural India
89. Dr Lal PathLabs: diagnostic labs fed by sample-collection centres

**Others (11)**
90. Indian Hotels (Taj): luxury hotels, now growing through management contracts
91. Havells: asset-light consumer electricals
92. Byju's: edtech that collapsed (cautionary tale)
93. SUGAR Cosmetics: D2C makeup brand moving into shops
94. The Souled Store: merchandise built on licensed pop-culture designs
95. Chaayos: chai café chain
96. Bira 91: beer brand that struggled (cautionary tale)
97. Udaan: B2B wholesale marketplace
98. Shiprocket: shipping and logistics software for small D2C sellers
99. Spinny: used cars with fixed prices and no haggling
100. HAL: government-owned defence manufacturer
